About

Autonomy needs
accountability.

AI agents are starting to move real money. The missing layer is not intelligence — it is authorization: who is this agent, what may it do, who answers for it, and how do you prove what happened. Regent exists to make that layer boring, inspectable infrastructure.

Product thesis

"Trust in autonomous finance will not come from smarter models. It will come from verifiable identity, enforced limits, and evidence anyone can check."

How we work

Three commitments.

Precision over promise

We label maturity

Devnet is called devnet. Roadmap is called roadmap. A certification is claimed only when the report exists. The public site is deliberately more precise than a pitch deck.

Evidence over trust

We minimize what you must believe

Audit evidence verifies against published keys and on-chain commitments. An auditor should not have to trust us — or our customers — to check what happened.

Fail closed

We refuse before we guess

If the control layer cannot decide, the money action does not happen. Uncertainty resolves toward the owner's protection, never toward throughput.

One control plane, three doors.

RFC 9421 in production

Agents on our marketplace authenticate by HTTP message signatures alone — self-registration, identity bootstrap and every purchase — no bearer keys to steal.

From the field

What a compliance lawyer asked after testing our agents.

AI
Aida IskakovaKYC/AML Compliance · AIFC regulatory framework · ex-Lead Legal Counsel (Regulatory Compliance), Halyk Bank
“During the Regent Safety Challenge every agent had its own Agent ID, connected tools and spending mandates. It made me ask the questions that matter: what makes an agent this agent — and if its model or tools change, is it still the same one? Who is it bound to? Who may grant it authority, change its limits, connect new tools? Six months from now, could you reconstruct which agent acted, on whose behalf, and with what authority at that moment? After testing, Know Your Agent finally made sense to me: it is not only what an agent did, but who acted, on whose behalf, and on what basis.”
