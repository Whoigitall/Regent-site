System map

Three layers, one decision path.

Settlement · Solana

On-chain programs

Three Anchor programs on devnet — agent-registry, mandate-registry, audit-anchor — hold agent identities, mandate commitments, and Merkle roots of audit batches.

Evidence · Celestia DA

Audit payload storage

Full audit payloads as data-availability blobs with Merkle proofs. What is committed cannot be silently altered; what is stored where is documented, layer by layer.

Control · Guardian

Risk engine

Rule engine plus behavioural scoring (Isolation Forest with SHAP explanations, drift detection) — allow, hold for a human, or stop.

Surface

Platform & SDK

Dashboard for operators — agents, mandates, audit log, kill switch — and a Python SDK plus REST API for integration.

Lifecycle

Before, at, and after execution.

Before

The agent is registered (AgentID), bound to a responsible party, and bounded by a mandate — amount, destination, time window, approval conditions. Nothing below runs without this.

At execution

Every financial action is authorized against the mandate and evaluated by Guardian. The deny happens before any money moves; the hold routes to a human operator.

After

The decision — allowed or denied — is hashed, Merkle-batched, and anchored. An auditor verifies against published keys and on-chain commitments, without trusting the operator.

Chain choices

Why Solana. Why Celestia.

Solana — settlement & anchoring

Per-transaction anchoring at fractions of a cent is what makes per-action compliance economically viable. Cost and finality are network characteristics of Solana, not Regent performance promises — current figures live in the network's own documentation.

Celestia — data availability

Dedicated DA for audit payloads (~$0.03–0.07 / MB, chain characteristic) with cryptographic guarantees on what was committed. Tamper-evidence is stated precisely: commitments are permanent; storage layers are documented per artifact.
